# Claude Design prompt — SureRide

Create a design canvas with **five artboards** for **SureRide**, a family-run cab company based in Jaipur, Rajasthan. Use the real content supplied below throughout — no lorem ipsum, no invented services.

## The business

SureRide runs its own fleet and its own drivers. It operates outstation, one-way, round-trip and local cab service across **Rajasthan, Delhi NCR, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh**. It has traded for years as *Jai Guru Dev Travels*, which stays as the heritage endorsement line; SureRide is the new public brand.

Customers are overwhelmingly **Hindi-speaking, on mid-range Android phones, on patchy 4G**. A meaningful share are families travelling to pilgrimage sites. A smaller share are foreign tourists and corporate bookers. **Design mobile-first** — the mobile artboard is not an afterthought.

Bookings happen by **phone call and WhatsApp**, not web forms. Those two actions must be reachable from every screen at every scroll position.

## Brand

- Wordmark: **SureRide**, one word, internal capital R. Always Latin script — never transliterated into Devanagari.
- Descriptor line: **Fixed fare. Named driver.**
- The recurring three-part device, used on every page: **Sure fare · Sure driver · Sure time**
- Hindi equivalent, used prominently: **किराया पक्का। ड्राइवर पक्का। टाइम पक्का।**
- Heritage line, small, in the footer and on About: *A Jai Guru Dev Travels company*

## Artboards

1. **Homepage — desktop** (1440 wide)
2. **Route page: Jaipur → Delhi — desktop** (1440) — the most important screen; most visitors land here, not on the homepage
3. **Route page: Jaipur → Delhi — mobile** (390 wide)
4. **Fares page — desktop** (1440)
5. **Drivers page — desktop** (1440)

## Visual direction

The trap to avoid is the default cab-website look: a stock sedan on a blue gradient, yellow taxi checkers, and a hero reading *Your Journey, Our Priority*. Equally wrong is the aggregator look — do not make this resemble Ola, Uber or Rapido. The brand name is already English and modern, so **the visual identity is the only thing signalling *local family firm, not app*. That is the differentiator; let it carry weight.**

Draw on Rajasthan with restraint — this is a booking utility that must feel trustworthy and fast, not a tourism poster.

**Palette**
- Indigo `#1B2A6B` — primary, headers, structure
- Marigold `#E8A33D` — call-to-action only, used sparingly
- Sandstone `#F4F1EC` — page ground
- Ink `#1A1D26` — text
- WhatsApp green `#127A56` — chat action only

**Type**
- Headings and fares: a confident, slightly condensed grotesk with humanist warmth — *not* a geometric tech sans, which reads startup.
- Fares and distances must use **tabular numerals**. A fare table with wobbling digits reads as untrustworthy.
- Devanagari is required from the start, not retrofitted. Noto Sans Devanagari pairs cleanly with most Latin grotesks.

**Motif**
The jharokha arch silhouette from Rajasthani architecture may frame route cards or section dividers — once or twice per page, as structure, never as wallpaper.

**Photography**
Use clearly-labelled placeholder blocks reading *"real fleet photo"* / *"real driver photo — phone snapshot"*. Do not use stock photography of smiling men in suits beside taxis — that is the single clearest visual signal of a broker front, and avoiding it is the point of the brand.

## Hard rules

- **The hero is a route picker, not a slogan.** From / To / Date / One-way or Round trip / Get fare — over a real fleet photograph. The first thing on the page is the thing the customer came to do.
- **Never mention brokers, middlemen or commissions anywhere on these screens.** The business does cut out brokers, but that is an internal rationale, not a customer need — leading with it answers a question nobody asked and plants a doubt. Sell the proof (fixed fare, named driver, owner's number) instead.
- **Sticky mobile action bar**: Call · WhatsApp · Get fare. Always visible, thumb-reachable.
- Phone number and WhatsApp in the header on every screen, tappable.
- Wide tables scroll inside their own container; the page never scrolls sideways.

## Screen content

### 1 — Homepage

**Header** SureRide wordmark · Routes · Services · Fleet · Fares · Drivers · Contact · phone number · WhatsApp button

**Hero** Route picker as above. Headline: **Cabs across Rajasthan, Delhi, Haryana and UP.** Subhead: *Fixed fare quoted in writing. Your driver's name and number before you travel.*

**Trust strip** (directly under hero) Sure fare · Sure driver · Sure time — with the Hindi line beneath in smaller type.

**Popular routes** — 8 cards, each showing route, approximate distance, and a "from ₹—" price:
Jaipur → Delhi (280 km) · Delhi → Jaipur (280 km) · Jaipur → Agra (240 km) · Jaipur → Khatu Shyam Ji (80 km) · Jaipur → Ajmer & Pushkar (135 km) · Jaipur → Udaipur (395 km) · Jaipur → Jodhpur (335 km) · Jaipur → Ranthambore (160 km)

**Services** — 6 tiles: One-way drop · Round trip · Airport transfer · Local 8hr/80km · Outstation · Tempo Traveller (12 & 17 seater)

**Fleet** — 5 cards: Swift Dzire (4 seats) · Ertiga (6) · Innova (7) · Innova Crysta (7) · Tempo Traveller (12–17)

**Pilgrimage circuit** — a distinct band: *Khatu Shyam · Salasar Balaji · Mehandipur Balaji · Rani Sati Dadi.* Set the heading in Hindi.

**Why book with us** — three points, no broker language: fare published in writing; driver's name, photo and number in advance; the owner's phone number, answered by a person.

**Reviews** — three Google review cards.

**Footer** — full address, GST number, transport registration, phone, WhatsApp, route links, service links, *A Jai Guru Dev Travels company*.

### 2 & 3 — Route page: Jaipur → Delhi

**H1** Jaipur to Delhi Taxi · **Sub** 280 km · about 5–6 hours via NH-48 · one-way and round trip

**Fare table** — vehicle rows, one-way and round-trip columns. *These figures are placeholders for layout only; the client supplies real fares:*
Swift Dzire ₹4,500 / ₹7,800 · Ertiga ₹5,500 / ₹9,200 · Innova ₹6,500 / ₹10,800 · Innova Crysta ₹7,500 / ₹12,500 · Tempo Traveller ₹11,000 / ₹18,000

Directly beneath, a clearly-set line: **Toll, parking, state tax and driver allowance are charged separately and listed before you book.**

**Booking block** — Call and WhatsApp buttons, prefilled *"Jaipur to Delhi"*.

**About the drive** — a real prose section: NH-48 the whole way, realistic timing, the good dhaba stops, toll count.

**Popular stops on this route** — Neemrana Fort, Behror, Manesar.

**FAQs** — use these exact questions:
- How long does the Jaipur to Delhi drive take?
- Are toll and state tax included in the fare?
- Can we stop at Neemrana on the way?
- Do you drop anywhere in Delhi NCR, including the airport?
- Will I get the driver's number before the trip?
- What happens if my flight is delayed?

**Two reviews** from this route, then **related routes**: Delhi → Jaipur · Jaipur → Agra · Jaipur → Gurgaon · Jaipur → Mathura.

The mobile artboard shows the same page with the sticky Call · WhatsApp · Get fare bar pinned at the bottom.

### 4 — Fares page

The transparency page. Per-km rate and daily minimum for each vehicle class; night charge; driver allowance; and a plain-language explanation of what toll, parking and state tax mean and when each applies. Design this to be **scannable and unambiguous** — it is the page that wins the booking when a customer is comparing quotes.

### 5 — Drivers page

A grid of driver cards: photo placeholder, first name, years driving, languages, routes they know best. *Placeholder examples — client supplies real drivers:* Ramesh — 14 years — Hindi, English — Jaipur–Delhi, Golden Triangle. Suresh — 9 years — Hindi — Rajasthan circuit, pilgrimage routes.

Open the page with a short line about why the drivers are named at all: you should know who is driving your family before they arrive.
